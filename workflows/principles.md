# Principles

The public checklist for this fleet pattern. Follow it when you run a desk from these pages, teach them, or adapt them.

Each line is here because the failure beside it costs a reread, a duplicated edit, or a private detail in a public file.

## How to use this list

Read it once before you add a desk. When a deliverable looks finished, spot-check the list the way you spot-check PROOF: the lines below, not the tone of the surrounding prose.

The worked versions live in [Soft Chief of Staff](soft-cos.md) and [Fleet board, empty wake, PROOF](fleet-board-empty-wake-proof.md). The picture is in [Fleet Architecture](architecture.md).

## The checklist

### 1. One bot, one job

**Failure:** Every desk answers, and two of them edit the same note.

**Rule:** Each desk has one job, written in a sentence, plus the anti-jobs next to it. The anti-jobs are the neighboring work this desk leaves to the owner.

### 2. Wrong desk, one line

**Failure:** The desk that received the message "helps" by doing half the other desk's job.

**Rule:** Name the owner desk in one line, then stop. If the friend only speaks to the GM, that line is the handoff the GM routes. The specialist still leaves the work alone.

### 3. One front door

**Failure:** The owner keeps a chat open per bot and repeats the same context in each.

**Rule:** A friend may talk only to the GM. The GM routes to the owner desk and does the routing job only.

### 4. The board holds ops truth

**Failure:** Yesterday's decision lives in a chat that the next session cannot see.

**Rule:** Start, block, and finish go on a shared markdown board. The next session reads the board before it asks anyone to repeat themselves.

### 5. Finish carries PROOF

**Failure:** A desk says "done" and the human has to reconstruct what finished, and what was supposed to stay untouched.

**Rule:** A finish has four fields: **task**, **done-when**, **evidence**, **don't**. Spot-check those fields.

### 6. Empty wake stays quiet

**Failure:** A scheduled check with no news posts "no change" until everyone mutes the channel.

**Rule:** Nothing material means no board write and no chat. Silence is the report.

### 7. Human notes stay as written

**Failure:** A desk rewrites a person's sentence to sound cleaner and the original decision disappears.

**Rule:** Append. Leave the human's lines intact.

### 8. Public pages stay public

**Failure:** A teaching doc includes a home-folder path, an agent id, a credential, or a dump of private notes.

**Rule:** Public examples use fictional desk names and relative paths such as `your-vault/` and `board.md`. Private notes, ids, and secrets stay off the public repo.

### 9. Mac-first sync is a pattern

**Failure:** A guide tells friends to open one specific person's machine folders.

**Rule:** The writing copy is the local vault, a git repo is the bridge for the slice you mean to keep, and a remote is the fallback other people can read. Name the pattern. Leave out the machine.

### 10. The coding course stays intact

**Failure:** A fleet update rewrites the lessons people already learned from.

**Rule:** The twelve Claude Code concepts remain the coding curriculum. Fleet pages sit beside them.

### 11. An unpublished pack is optional

**Failure:** Friends are told they cannot start until they obtain someone's private setup.

**Rule:** These public pages are enough to copy the pattern. A starter, if you publish one later, presents the job, the anti-jobs, the redirect line, and an empty board. It is additional, and it is free of private contents.

### 12. Share with attribution

**Failure:** A remix drops the author and the license, or adds terms that block sharing.

**Rule:** CC-BY-SA 4.0. Credit Terry Chan / Terrium, link the license, and share adaptations under the same terms.
